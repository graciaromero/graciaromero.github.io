Software solutions

CIMMYT Maize Scanner

During my PhD, I participated in the conception and development of software solutions designed to overcome the "data analysis bottleneck" in UAV-based phenotyping. Beyond the use of spectral sensors, we explored the potential of conventional RGB cameras as an alternative to calculate Vegetation Indices. Our first objectives were to implement and develop different strategies to evaluate canopy greenness across different color models. To make these tools accessible to the broader research community, we integrated these protocols into a specialized ImageJ/Fiji plugin.

A key milestone of this work was the development of an early prototype in collaboration with CIMMYT, providing breeders with a standardized, user-friendly tool for field data analysis. The CIMMYT Maize Scanner is an open-source high-throughput phenotyping (HTP) tool designed for agricultural research and plant breeding. Rather than being a physical scanning machine, it is a software suite that processes digital images to extract color-based indices related to plant health and performance.

Key Indices Extracted:

  • Green Area (GA): The fraction of pixels within the green color range, used to estimate total vegetation cover.
  • Greener Area (GGA): A more restrictive index that excludes yellowish-green tones to better differentiate active photosynthetic biomass from senescing (aging) tissue.
  • Crop Senescence Index (CSI): Calculated from GA and GGA to measure the rate of plant aging or stress-induced leaf death.
  • Color Space Indices: Metrics for Hue, Saturation, Lightness, and other color dimensions (e.g., a*, b*, u*, v*).

The scanner is primarily used to replace or supplement subjective visual scoring for yield evaluation, disease screening, and nitrogen and water stress monitoring.

The project was led by Prof. Shawn Carlisle Kefauver at the University of Barcelona (Copyright 2015) as part of a collaboration with CIMMYT to streamline field data collection.
